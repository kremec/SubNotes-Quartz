- Social media is full of "hot takes", that are not well backed up - when discussing why something is good or bad, take into account that the opinion can be based on not understanding the topic enough.
- Its much harder to get the dopamine effect when reading rather than scrolling social media or even programming, which is still very stimulant.
- The brain over time optimises for consuming shorter media, but you need maturity to know when you need to do the right thing, even if it is difficult.
- Avoid consuming "brainless" passive media where you don't need to think anything out - e.g. most of TV - instead, think through, push into the ideas and argue the topics.
	TV is for family movie night, but any other screen time is either videogames, programming, ... which are more thought-filled media

- When not motivated to do anything, you are initially rewarded but quickly feeling bad after consuming short media.
- Notice the pattern (you don't need to do anything about it initially), just see it. After a while, you can naturally break out of it as your subconciousness grows bored by it.
- Before getting yourself to do something productive that you don't want to start the and there, write out your feelings about it, and repeat it after getting into the flow - you'll see how your perception of the task changes.
- Accept your feelings are temporary, and as such you shold not think too much about them when deciding "Should I do ..."
	A lot of people use therapy as a replacement for community - connections/bonds to people around them. We are social creatures, and most of the people going to therapy just lack the community around them.

- We should think big, as only thinking big (after dealing with lacking motivation) leads to big discoveries.
