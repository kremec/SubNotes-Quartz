A study on (a fairly small) population of young software engineers (in their 20's) was conducted, coming to following observations:
- Informal communication: keeping communication to the point with no ballast
- Wanting to not be underestimated: mostly, older collegues are right as they have more experience on the field
  Instead of seeking more responsibility, try to do standout, exemplary work - start looking at problems, taling to senior engineers and presenting ideas
- Bias on modern technology: you should not be running into each and every niche new thing that looks promising, but is dead within a year
- Work with purpose: very rarely work gives you purpose to life, but rather satisfaction. Work should rarely be a core identity
- Not being able to do enjoyable programming for the soul in free time, as you sit every day in front of computers at the job: just try to open the editor, after the first 5 minutes you will fall into the Flow
- Having a good manager who cares for team members: not limited to only Gen Z, but all generations
- Senior engineers are often more opinionated on certain implementation plan: they have seen lots of examples of good and bad practices, but it also slows down adoption of newer practices (you dont fix what's not broken)
You want an energy and enthusiasm of an intern/junior and the calmness and experiencd of a senior engineer, to mix it together
